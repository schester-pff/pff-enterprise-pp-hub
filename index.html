What to focus on

  • Player accuracy over precision. Get the correct 22 players entered. Don't get stuck on marginal positional nuances — some ambiguity is expected this early.
  • Don't rely on numbers alone. Use secondary cues — gloves, socks, wrists, arms, legs, body shape, running style. Take shorthand notes (e.g. "LWS" for long white socks).
  • Don't stress about speed. Never sacrifice accuracy for speed. Your first game may take a long time — that's completely normal.
  • Build a routine. Group skill players together, then QB and OL. Pick a consistent method and the more plays you do in a sitting, the faster you'll learn.

Dashboard / Rules & Conduct

Rules & conduct

These are the rules of the program. They're straightforward, and most of them come down to one thing: do the work, on time, to the best of your ability. Read them before Week 0 — being removed from the course is almost always avoidable, and it starts with knowing where the lines are.

Start here
Getting things wrong is expected+
This is the most important thing to understand before anything else. Getting things wrong is not only okay, it's expected. Your feedback sheets will contain hundreds of errors, especially early on, and that is completely fine. We are not looking for perfection. We are looking for you to follow the game, try to get the data right, learn from your mistakes, and show that you're progressing — even if you're still making plenty of mistakes along the way. None of the rules below are about punishing you for being wrong. They're about effort, honesty, and meeting your deadlines.
What you have to do each week
Complete the full assignment, on the correct side+
Each week you must complete the entire assignment given to you — every play listed, all the way through — charting the correct side of the game. Your first two games are half-game assignments and your last two are full games; either way, you must complete all of the plays you've been set. Doing only part of your assignment is treated the same as not doing it: if you don't finish everything assigned in time and you don't have an approved extension, you fail the week and you're out. Your week pages list exactly which side to chart, the plays to complete, and the DAT filters to set.
Hit your deadline+
Every game has a deadline of 9am ET on the Monday that closes the week. Miss it without an approved extension and you're removed from the program. Don't leave your game to the last minute — give yourself enough room to finish properly and to fix anything that needs fixing before the deadline.
Only submit the completion form when you're genuinely done+
Submitting your game completion form triggers our review — there is no undo. Do not fill in the form until you have actually finished the game; submitting early, by mistake, or before you've completed all the plays can't be reversed. (For how your work is checked and when feedback arrives, see the FAQ.)
Attend the mandatory drop-in sessions+
Each week includes mandatory drop-in sessions. You don't need to attend every one — there are several across the week — but by the end of each week you must have attended enough to satisfy the mandatory requirement. If you attend none of a week's mandatory sessions, or miss three mandatory sessions in a row, you'll be removed from the program.
Good faith vs bad faith
What counts as a good-faith submission+
A good-faith submission is one where it's clear you followed the flow of the game and genuinely tried to enter the correct data, play by play. You can get players wrong, get positions wrong, and miss roles — and as long as it's clear you were trying, that is a good-faith submission. This is what the vast majority of trainees do, and it's exactly what we want. Honest mistakes are never a problem.
What counts as a bad-faith submission+
A bad-faith submission is any game where it's clear you made little or no effort to fill in the data accurately to the best of your ability. The obvious signs are: locking and saving the same 22 players in the same positions play after play (with how often formations change and players sub in and out, this is very obvious across multiple plays); consistently failing to enter any of the role data — bunch, press, blitz, pass block — where missing the majority shows you didn't attempt them; and leaving plays blank or missed out altogether. A repetitive pattern of the exact same mistakes, where it's clear you weren't really engaging with the game, also counts. To be clear: getting things wrong while genuinely trying is not bad faith — it's expected. Bad faith is about not trying.
Charting the wrong side of the game+
Each assignment tells you exactly which side to chart. Charting the wrong side isn't bad faith — you tried — but it's a serious problem, because we cannot run feedback on a side you weren't assigned, which makes the submission worthless to you and to us. If you realise before the deadline, you can go back and complete the correct side. But if you submit the wrong side and only discover it when we review your game — too late to have redone it — you fail the week. This is one more reason to submit early enough to fix mistakes.
Extensions
You get one 48-hour extension — here's how it works+
Everyone is allowed a single 48-hour extension, which can be used any time between Week 1 and Week 3. It cannot be used for the PFFU week (Week 0) or for the final test game (Week 4). Once you've used it, that's it — you do not get another, and asking for a second will result in removal from the course. All extension requests must be made using the extension request form, available in the Hub and in the #pp-forms channel on Discord, and must be submitted before the deadline. If you don't request it properly before the deadline, you don't get one. Requesting a second extension after you've already used yours will take you off the course.
Conduct
Do your own work — no collusion+
This is a competitive process, and you should treat it as one. Colluding with another trainee or copying their work will remove you both from the course. Beyond the rule, it's worth understanding why it makes no sense: by trying to pull someone across the finish line, you hurt both of you. They won't be able to lean on you in season, and you may be handing away a place that could have been someone else's — maybe even yours. We want the best to rise to the top. Let them.
Ask questions to understand, not to get answers+
Use Discord and your trainers to understand a play, a position, or a concept — not to get the answer to who player X is on play Y. Asking others to simply hand you answers undermines the whole point of training and, again, hurts you: you need to be able to do this on your own in season. Engage to learn, not to shortcut.
Protect our material+
Sharing any of our proprietary material with anyone outside the training group will remove you from the course. The guides, the film, the documents and the data are ours — keep them within the program.
Follow the Discord server rules+
Breaking any of our Discord server rules while you're on the server will remove you from the course. The full server rules are on the Discord page — read them.
Don't go quiet+
If you simply vanish, you'll miss a game deadline — and missing a deadline without an approved extension means you're out. Life happens, and that's what the extension is for, but you have to use it properly and in advance. Don't disappear.
What removal means
Being removed — and what it means for your pay+
Removal from the program is immediate. The things that will remove you: a submission deemed to be bad faith; missing a deadline without an approved 48-hour extension; charting the wrong side and submitting too late to fix it; failing to complete your full assignment; breaking the Discord server rules; sharing our proprietary material outside the group; failing to satisfy the mandatory drop-in attendance; or colluding with or copying another trainee. One more thing to be clear about: if you're removed, you are not paid for that week — no matter how much of the game you did or didn't complete.

Dashboard / FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the most common questions. Still stuck? Ask Coach, or check the #pp-faq channel on Discord before posting a question.

Pay & training structure
Is training paid?+
Week 0 is PFFU — an e-learning self-assessment that is unpaid. From Week 1 onwards, every training game is paid. The two half games pay $25 each and the two full games pay $50 each, so $150 in total across the four games.
When are we paid during training?+
You're paid twice during training — once after Week 2, and again at the end of the program.
What's the difference between a half game and a full game?+
A half game means you chart one half only — Game 1 is the first half of Ohio State vs Michigan. A full game means all four quarters. Half games pay $25 and full games pay $50.
What's the workload over the summer?+
Around 10 hours per week. The program is self-paced from home and includes both mandatory drop-in sessions and additional voluntary ones each week.
What does "Week 0" mean?+
Week 0 is your PFFU self-assessment week — the prep week before the games begin. Week 1 is then Game 1, Week 2 is Game 2, and so on, so the week number and game number always line up.
Why is my deadline 9am ET Monday and not Sunday night?+
Deadlines are 9am ET on Monday rather than late Sunday night. The week effectively ends Sunday, but the Monday morning cut-off gives you a little breathing room first thing.
What happens during the rest week?+
There's a rest week for the July 4th holiday after the Game 2 deadline. No assignment and no new material that week — Game 3 lands when Week 3 opens.
What happens if I miss a deadline or don't complete a game?+
If you fail to complete a game, miss the submission deadline, or submit a game where it's clear no genuine effort was made, you won't be paid for that game and will be removed from the program. We take deadlines seriously — this is a professional operation and the data has real consequences for the teams that rely on it.
The work & the tools
Which DAT role should I select?+
Always select "Player Participation Data" — never "Player Participation Sheets". Each week's page lists the exact filters to set as well.
Can I rewatch plays as many times as I need?+
Yes. Precision matters more than speed, and rewinding in VLC whenever you're uncertain is the right approach. You should never be guessing.
How do I watch the game film during training?+
During training, the video files live in the Google Drive film folder for each game. Download the file and play it back in VLC media player — you can pause, rewind and replay as much as you need. Important: the video player built into the DAT does not work during training, so always work from the downloaded file in VLC.
Do I need a sports package or TV subscription?+
No. All the game film you need is provided in the Google Drive film folders — no TV subscription, sports package or external login required.
What software and hardware do I need?+
You'll need Google Drive, VLC media player and a Discord account. No specialist software beyond that, and no VPN. There's no formal hardware spec — a computer that can handle video playback and an internet connection fast enough to stream comfortably is all you need.
How long does a full game take?+
It varies, but experienced collectors often land in the 4–6 hour range. During training you'll naturally be slower, and that's expected — speed develops significantly over time, so where you start is not a concern to us.
Do we work individually or in teams?+
Individually. Each collector works their own assigned games independently.
Feedback & getting better
How is my work checked, and how do I get feedback?+
Each game you complete in training is compared against the same game charted by our training team. The copy yours is compared to is at least 99.8% accurate — effectively error-free. Anywhere your entry differs from ours, you receive an error for that play. Within minutes of submitting your game completion form, a feedback sheet is generated highlighting every error from that game, and you'll get an email letting you know it's ready. Go through it carefully to understand what went wrong, and note any plays or issues you want to raise with a trainer.
How much football knowledge do I need?+
A basic understanding of the game. This is a data collection role, not a football education program — for most people the learning curve is about the data, not the sport.
What separates trainees who excel from those who just pass?+
The ones who stand out treat training as a real performance environment, not a test to pass. They rewatch when uncertain, engage properly with feedback rather than just noting their score, and build consistent habits early. Those who rush through or plateau after initial feedback tend not to carry it through to the season.
How do I get the most out of training?+
Attend the drop-in sessions. Engage with your feedback — don't just look at the number, understand the mistake. Ask questions when something isn't clear. And treat every training game as a genuine performance assessment, because it is.
After training
Does completing training guarantee in-season work?+
No. Acceptance into training isn't a job offer — the Academy is the evaluation. How you perform across the program is how you earn your place for the season. PFF Enterprise operates as a meritocracy, and only those who excel will be brought on for in-season work.
Is there a path to a full-time role?+
Yes. Every full-time Data Analyst at PFF Enterprise has come through the contractor pool, and we hire full-time staff every year. It's not a guarantee, but it's a real pathway that multiple people take every season.

PFF Enterprise Discord server rules

Please read and follow these rules to keep our community professional, safe and respectful.

Respect & professionalism

Be kind and respectful to everyone. No harassment, discrimination, bullying, hate speech or offensive behaviour. Keep conversations respectful and work-appropriate, and avoid heated debates or controversial topics.

Reporting issues

If someone is making you uncomfortable or breaking the rules, DM a trainer privately.

No spam or self-promotion

No spamming or promoting yourself or others without management approval. This includes ads, server invites and unsolicited DMs. Selling goods, services or any commercial activity is not allowed.

Content restrictions

No NSFW, violent, graphic or otherwise inappropriate content — this covers text, images, emojis, nicknames and links.

Membership

This server is only for current PFF Enterprise Data Collectors and Trainees. Anyone else will be removed. If you leave PFF Enterprise or go inactive, your access may be changed or removed.

Getting help from trainers

Trainers are here to help. Ask questions respectfully, and avoid spam-tagging or repeating messages.

Channel usage & conduct

Post in the correct channels and move off-topic discussion to where it belongs. In voice channels, stay professional, minimise background noise and use push-to-talk if needed.

Nicknames & identity

Please use your real name as your nickname so we can assign roles properly.

Confidentiality

Don't share company data or anyone's personal information without consent. Respect everyone's privacy.

Technical integrity

Never upload files or content intended to crash or harm Discord or other people's devices.
